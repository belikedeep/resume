# Resume Guidelines (Complete)

---

## Make Your Resume Easy to Read and Skim

- Use a single-column layout
- Avoid icons, images, and graphics
- Do not indent sections and bullet points. Using bullet points inherently creates indentation
- Use bullet points, not paragraphs
- Use a modern, easy-to-read font like Calibri, Bitstream Charter, or Arial
- Use a black (not thin) font with at least 10.5+ font size
- Don't justify your text since it creates inconsistent spacing between words
- Ensure you have enough white space. No cramming. Use sufficient margins (minimum 0.4 inches) and line spacing (minimum 1.07)
- Avoid excessive italicization, bolding, and ALL CAPS. If you do use one, use it independently of each other
- Have clear section separation. The reader should be able to easily distinguish sections from others
- One page long, unless you have some 10+ years of experience. The rule of thumb is 1 page per decade of experience
- Separate your skills using commas

---

## Make Your Resume More Effective

- Include relevant and technical details to showcase your qualifications and distinguish yourself from other candidates
- Provide context and incorporate relevant keywords
- Avoid adding unnecessary information that doesn’t add to your candidacy
- Bring your critical and pertinent details to the top and the left of the resume
- A resume functions as a 30-second elevator pitch on paper

---

## Keep It to 1 Page

- Most resumes do not need a second page
- Multi-page resumes often contain irrelevant or poorly formatted content
- Hiring teams spend less effort on longer resumes
- Remove less relevant content if needed
- Use additional content in a portfolio instead

---

## Resume Principles (ATS-Friendly)

- Easily skimmable by recruiters, hiring managers, and interviewers
- Easily parsable by an Application Tracking System (ATS)

---

## ATS

- Used to scan resumes and extract structured data
- Match keywords with job descriptions
- Requires simple, clean formatting

---

## ATS Myths

- ATS does not randomly reject resumes
- Fancy design does not improve chances
- Keywords matter but context matters more
- Humans still review resumes
- Over-optimization can hurt readability

## Typography & Layout Rules

- Use a modern, easy-to-read font like Calibri, Bitstream Charter, Arial, Lato, or Helvetica
- Don't use grey-colored fonts since they're hard to read. Stick to black
- Ensure sufficient contrast with white background
- Don't justify text
- Don't italicize text
- Don't bold keywords within bullet points
- Use bold/italics/ALL CAPS sparingly and independently
- Don't indent sections or bullet points
- Separate sections clearly
- Ensure consistent white space
- Ensure margins minimum 0.4 inches

---

## Date Formatting Rules

- Use Present instead of Current, Now, or Ongoing
- Use consistent month format (e.g. March 2022, not Winter 2022)
- Do not include specific days
- Do not abbreviate years
- Do not use numeric months
- Proper abbreviations: Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, June, July, Aug, Sept, Oct, Nov, Dec
- No periods in abbreviations
- Use en dash (–) for ranges
- Include space around dash
- Right align dates
- Bullet points should not exceed date alignment

---

## Section Order Guidelines

### If working full-time

- Work Experience > Skills > Education
- Skills > Work Experience > Education

### If student/new grad

- Education > Work Experience > Skills
- Education > Work Experience > Projects > Skills

### If no technical experience

- Education > Projects > Work Experience > Skills

### If no work experience

- Education > Projects > Volunteer Experience/Extracurriculars > Skills

---

## Additional Rules

- No summary/profile unless senior/staff, career switch, or gap
- No references section

---

## Contact Information

- Include security clearance if applicable
- Include citizenship/visa status if needed

### Do NOT include

- Address or ZIP code
- Location unless required
- LinkedIn (optional and often unnecessary)
- Phone number (optional)

### Email

- Use one email
- Use Gmail or Outlook
- Avoid outdated providers

### Links

- Do not mask links
- Use plain text:
  - name@gmail.com
  - github.com/username
  - myportfolio.com
- Do not prefix labels
- Do not include https://www
- Do not style links

---

## Work Experience Section

- Name section: Work Experience or Experience
- Only include paid experience
- Include research if applicable
- Clearly label internships and contracts
- Order by relevance or impact
- Tailor for each job
- Provide context and problem-solving details
- Focus on accomplishments, not duties
- Emphasize engineering/problem-solving skills over tools
- Use bullet points
- New grads: prioritize fundamentals over leadership

---

## Bullet Point Guidelines

### Objectives

- Highlight technical work
- Highlight challenges overcome
- Highlight impact

---

### Rules

- Bullet points should be 1–2 lines long. Use Quillbot and LanguageTool to help you paraphrase and shorten your bullets
- Aim for 1 sentence per bullet
- Bullet points should be ordered from most relevant/impressive to least (order by impact), as some hiring managers only have time to read the first they should get THE BEST!
- Don't use personal pronouns i.e. I, we, us, my, our, their
- Don't end bullet points with periods. Bullet points != sentences
- Your bullet points shouldn't go further right than your dates
- Don't spill bullets onto the following line with only 1–4 words on it. It's an extreme waste of space. For example https://imgur.com/QCcZ792
- Don't allow words to wrap to the next line and get hyphenated
- Avoid the excess use of sub-bullet points. They can clutter your resume, making it more verbose and harder to read
- Avoid using apostrophes ', ampersands &, and slashes /
- Avoid the excessive use of adjectives and adverbs, they're superfluous:
  - B-adjectives: excellent, innovative, expert, revolutionary, disruptive
  - B-adverbs: creatively, diligently, meticulously, strategically, successfully, independently, innovatively, excellently, expertly
- Use digits instead of spelling out numbers: 8 eight

---

## Action Verbs

- Each bullet should begin with a strong, past-tense action verb: https://web.archive.org/web/20240515003325/https://www.engineering.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/users/user240/Action%20Words%20for%20ENG%20(website).pdf
- Led is the past tense of lead

### Good Examples

- analyzed, architected, automated, built, created, decreased, designed, developed, implemented, improved, optimized, published, reduced, refactored

### Bad Examples

- aided, assisted, coded, collaborated, communicated, executed, exposed to, gained experience, helped, participated, programmed, ran, used, utilized, worked on
- Don't use the verb utilize. 9/10 times that word will be attached to some disgusting Gordian knot of a sentence that says in 20 words what could be said in 10.
- Superfluous/awkward/unnecessarily complex verbs to avoid: amplified, conceptualized, crafted, elevated, employed, engaged, engineered, enhanced, ensured, fostered, headed, honed, innovated, mastered, orchestrated, perfected, pioneered, revolutionized, spearheaded, transformed
- Frequently misused verbs to avoid: leverage, enhance, utilize

## Bullet Frameworks

### STAR

- Situation Task Action Result

### XYZ

- Accomplished X as measured by Y, by doing Z

### CAR

- Challenge Action Result

- Move metrics to the start when possible

## Brainstorming

- What did I do?
- How did I do it?
- Why did I do it?
- What were the results?

---

## Writing Exercise

- Recall project goals
- Identify how goals were achieved
- Identify if exceeded
- Write everything
- Then refine

---

## Integration

- Do not list components only
- Explain how systems work together
- Show technical depth

---

## Sensitive Content

- Avoid confidential details
- Generalize descriptions
- Show capability without revealing specifics.
  - *Example*: Instead of "I developed C++ software for testing LANTIRN display pod software used on the F-14D Tomcat", use "I developed C++ software for evaluating aircraft subsystems on [some hardware standard]-based test bench" or "I created a proof-of-concept integrating COTS items into a data recorder".
  - *Example*: Instead of revealing exact composite layups and procedures, use "implemented a new standard process used to form composite parts, reducing lead times by 33%".

---

## Final Principle

- Focus on clarity, impact, and readability
